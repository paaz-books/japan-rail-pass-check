What each option costs

    It's close: separates the two cheapest

    Within about 10 percent, the difference is the price of what gives you. Three questions decide it; if your answers split, take the cheaper option.

    1. Will you add rides as you go? A pass makes extra rides inside its area and days free.
    2. Do you need the fastest trains at the times you want? The Japan Rail Pass leaves out the Nozomi and Mizuho unless you buy special tickets (¥4,960 between Tokyo and Kyoto). Between Tokyo and Osaka, up to 12 Nozomi an hour run at busy times, against 2 Hikari.
    3. How much buying and booking will you put up with? A pass is one purchase, picked up with your passport; tickets mean one booking per long ride.

    How the check works

    Single tickets add up every ride. For each pass, the check finds the run of consecutive days, as long as the pass lasts, whose covered rides cost the most as tickets, and starts the pass there. The pass option is the pass price for everyone plus the rides outside those days as tickets.

    • Rides on the Tokaido Shinkansen are priced as Nozomi tickets. The Japan Rail Pass covers them on the slower Hikari.
    • The Japan Rail Pass covers only the JR part of the Fuji Excursion, as far as Otsuki. The JR Tokyo Wide Pass covers the whole ride.
    • The Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass doesn't cover the shinkansen between Kyoto and Shin-Osaka; from Kyoto, you ride the JR Kyoto Line to Shin-Osaka first.
    • Children's fares are half the adult fare, rounded down to ¥10, which can differ from JR's own rounding by a few yen.
    • Most of these passes are sold only to foreign visitors who enter Japan as tourists.

    Fares are for adults, one way, with a reserved seat in the regular season, checked on September 30, 2026 on operator pages and route searches. Reserved seats cost ¥200 less on quiet days and ¥200 to ¥400 more in busy seasons. Check the operator's site before you buy. Your rides are saved in this browser only.
